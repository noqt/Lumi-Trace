## 1. Status and authority

- **Version:** V0.3.1
- **Version title:** Governed Natural Corpus Intake and Baseline
- **Primary objective:** Admit, label, split, and evaluate a natural Trace Code
  pilot corpus
- **Trace IR:** Preserved unchanged; no expansion in V0.3.1
- **Training:** Not authorised
- **Model acquisition:** Not authorised
- **Protected holdback:** Must remain unopened
- **GPU requirement:** None

This brief authorises and bounds the acquisition and private evaluation of
public, permissively licensed natural repositories and their public security
evidence for the V0.3 Trace Code pilot.

Authority is limited to:

- identifying eligible public repositories;
- acquiring immutable repository revisions through a controlled intake path;
- recording licence, rights, provenance, and lineage;
- constructing controlled-reviewed source-location labels;
- creating vulnerable, fixed, safe, and hard-negative groups;
- assigning repository-disjoint development and qualification partitions;
- running the exact V0.1 deterministic runtime through Trace-Eval;
- sealing and scoring natural development and qualification results; and
- retaining private evidence plus disclosure-safe public summaries.

This brief does not authorise:

- customer, partner, or private repository intake;
- unpublished or embargoed vulnerability material;
- CyberGym tasks;
- historical Lumi or Lumi Scout evidence;
- protected-holdback opening;
- live telemetry or Trace IR expansion;
- foundation-model or comparator-weight download;
- `TRACE-001` training or fine-tuning;
- exploit generation;
- repository build or test execution during intake;
- public-target scanning;
- model-weight publication; or
- publication of third-party repository contents or derived source excerpts.

## 2. Reason for V0.3.1

V0.3 completed the required contracts, policies, metrics, controlled fixtures,
and public-safe evidence seal. It did not admit a natural corpus:

| Measure | V0.3 observed | V0.3 pilot target |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Accepted natural groups | 0 | 50-100 |
| Accepted natural repositories | 0 | 8-12 |
| Natural development runs | 0 | At least one sealed baseline |
| Natural qualification runs | 0 | One, if preconditions pass |

The primary programme blocker is now corpus admission, not evaluator
implementation.

V0.3.1 must not add architecture merely to create activity. It must use the
existing V0.3 evaluator to obtain the missing natural evidence.

## 3. Mission

V0.3.1 must answer:

1. Can 8 to 12 unrelated natural repositories be admitted with clear
   evaluation rights, immutable provenance, and repository-lineage separation?
2. Can 50 to 100 useful candidate-ranking groups be labelled without deriving
   truth from Trace output?
3. Does the unchanged V0.1 snapshot and index contain the accepted vulnerable
   implementation locations?
4. Does the current deterministic ranker distinguish vulnerable
   implementations from observations, harnesses, witnesses, fix-only sites,
   and natural lexical decoys?
5. What false-vulnerability and unsafe non-abstention profile appears on
   matched safe revisions and controls?
6. How dependent is ranking performance on explicit paths, symbols, and other
   answer-like finding cues?
7. Are the pilot labels, controls, splits, metrics, and resource costs adequate
   to justify scaling toward 500 groups and 25 unrelated future-training
   repositories?
8. Does the evidence support deterministic remediation, a narrowly scoped
   future learned reranker, or no model work?

The required outcome is a trustworthy natural baseline or a precise,
evidence-backed stop.

## 4. Governing decisions

### 4.1 Use the unchanged V0.1 runtime first

The first natural baseline must use the exact V0.1 wheel already identified by
Trace-Eval. Do not revise:

- ranking weights;
- test-path penalties;
- symbol extraction;
- finding normalisation;
- top-k behavior;
- location-role matching;
- abstention logic; or
- metric definitions

before the first natural development outputs are sealed.

Any later deterministic remediation must receive a new runtime identity and be
reported as a separate condition.

### 4.2 This is a pilot, not the final training corpus

The V0.3.1 target is 50 to 100 groups across 8 to 12 repositories. The existing
training-readiness targets remain:

- at least 500 useful labelled groups;
- at least 25 unrelated future-training repositories;
- repository-disjoint development and holdback partitions;
- meaningful natural hard negatives;
- audited location and reproduction labels; and
- adequate deterministic candidate recall under an approved threshold.

Pilot groups may later become eligible for a future-training-candidate
partition only after a separate training-rights and exposure review. Local
evaluation admission does not establish training eligibility.

### 4.3 Safe discrimination is primary

The pilot must not maximise vulnerable examples while neglecting safe
controls. It must test whether Trace can avoid:

- labelling a fixed revision as vulnerable;
- ranking a reproduction harness as the vulnerable implementation;
- treating a crash site as proof of upstream causality;
- following a stale or misleading path;
- ranking a lexical decoy above the accepted target;
- converting insufficient evidence into `SUPPORTED`; and
- accepting malformed or unsupported input.

Recall may not compensate for a safety-floor violation.

### 4.4 No natural source enters the public repository

Repository contents, findings, labels, manifests, raw outputs, review receipts,
and case-level results remain in governed private storage.

The public repository may retain only:

- code and schemas;
- owned synthetic fixtures;
- counts;
- aggregate metrics;
- non-sensitive identities;
- disclosure-safe decisions; and
- a boundary-reviewed evidence seal.

## 5. Intake rights policy

### 5.1 Initial licence boundary

To minimise ambiguity, the pilot should prefer repositories distributed under
clearly identified permissive licences such as:

- Apache-2.0;
- MIT;
- BSD-2-Clause;
- BSD-3-Clause;
- ISC; or
- another licence explicitly approved for this intake.

Licence identifiers must be confirmed from the exact acquired revision. A
repository name, hosting-platform label, search result, or current default
branch is not sufficient evidence.

Repositories with unclear, conflicting, source-available-only, bespoke, or
missing licences must fail closed unless separately reviewed.

### 5.2 Separate rights dimensions

Each repository and group must record these independently:

- local evaluation permitted;
- retention permitted;
- derived metadata retention permitted;
- public aggregate reporting permitted;
- source redistribution permitted;
- label redistribution permitted; and
- future model-training use reviewed.

`future model-training use reviewed` defaults to `NO`.

An open-source licence must not be represented as an automatic weight licence
or a completed training-data legal review.

### 5.3 Eligible security evidence

Security evidence may include:

- public maintainer security commits;
- public advisories;
- public CVE records;
- repository regression tests with clear security semantics;
- release notes;
- publicly documented fixing revisions;
- locally inspectable vulnerable/fixed revision pairs; and
- an optional locally reproduced witness under a separately qualified
  reproduction plan.

The evidence must identify immutable versions. A mutable web page may inform
intake but cannot be the sole identity anchor.

### 5.4 Exclusions

Reject:

- private vulnerability reports;
- embargoed fixes;
- leaked or stolen source;
- uncertain access rights;
- customer findings;
- repositories requiring credentialed access;
- data obtained through circumvention;
- malicious packages acquired for execution;
- model or dataset repositories that require remote code execution to inspect;
- repositories whose licence cannot be tied to the acquired revision; and
- cases whose label depends only on an unverified vulnerability claim.

## 6. Controlled acquisition

### 6.1 Acquisition register

Before fetching content, create an intake proposal containing:

- proposed repository identity;
- canonical upstream URL;
- hosting provider;
- requested revisions;
- licence evidence location;
- security evidence references;
- expected language and CWE strata;
- project family and known fork lineage;
- proposed use;
- expected retention;
- operator and decision identity; and
- acquisition state.

Permitted acquisition states:

- `PROPOSED`
- `RIGHTS_PRECHECK_PASSED`
- `ACQUISITION_APPROVED`
- `ACQUIRED_UNADMITTED`
- `ADMITTED_FOR_EVALUATION`
- `REJECTED`
- `RETIRED`

Only `ACQUISITION_APPROVED` records may be fetched.

### 6.2 Inert acquisition procedure

Acquisition must not execute repository-controlled behavior.

The procedure must:

- use an approved Git or archive transport;
- pin the exact upstream and revision;
- disable recursive submodule acquisition;
- disable Git LFS smudge or other checkout filters;
- disable repository hooks;
- avoid package installation;
- avoid build, test, generator, and setup execution;
- avoid remote includes;
- acquire only required revisions;
- record transport and content hashes;
- scan for symlinks, special files, unsafe paths, and excessive size;
- materialise a clean snapshot through the existing Trace repository boundary;
  and
- retain an acquisition receipt.

Submodules, vendored dependencies, generated assets, and large binary artifacts
must be admitted separately or remain inert references.

### 6.3 Immutable repository identity

Each admitted revision must bind:

- upstream URL;
- version-control commit or release identity;
- acquired object or archive hash;
- Trace tree identity;
- licence file hash;
- acquisition receipt;
- lineage and family identifiers;
- vulnerable/fixed state;
- retention location; and
- admission decision.

Mutable clone paths and branch names are observational, not identity anchors.

## 7. Corpus construction

### 7.1 Pilot targets

The admitted pilot should contain:

- 8 to 12 unrelated repositories;
- 50 to 100 accepted primary-metric groups;
- vulnerable and safe states;
- multiple repository families;
- multiple supported implementation languages;
- multiple CWE families;
- multiple label-evidence methods;
- natural hard negatives;
- finding-location ambiguity; and
- sufficient strata to expose obvious evaluator or corpus-design defects.

The corpus need not be balanced artificially across every dimension. Actual
distributions must be reported, and missing strata must remain visible.

### 7.2 Unit of evaluation

One candidate-ranking group binds:

- one immutable repository revision;
- one governed finding input;
- one accepted label set;
- one or more accepted targets;
- target location roles;
- vulnerable, fixed, safe, or insufficient-evidence state;
- hard-negative and safe-control metadata;
- rights and provenance;
- split and exposure state;
- controlled-review receipts; and
- optional reproduction truth.

Transformed variants of one natural case remain one lineage cluster. They must
not inflate repository independence or primary group counts.

### 7.3 Vulnerable and fixed pairs

Where the evidence permits, retain:

- the vulnerable parent or pre-fix revision;
- the fixing revision;
- the fixed or post-fix state;
- the security-relevant diff;
- the accepted vulnerable implementation;
- fix-only locations;
- matched safe locations; and
- revision-pair identity.

The fixing diff informs label construction but must not be exposed to the Trace
runner.

### 7.4 Useful-group criteria

A group is useful only if:

- rights and provenance pass;
- the repository identity is reproducible;
- the finding contains enough evidence to define a legitimate task;
- the accepted target is supported independently of Trace output;
- the target can be expressed in the V0.3 location-role contract;
- ambiguity is either resolved or explicitly excluded;
- its lineage and split are known;
- it contributes a relevant positive, negative, or safety-control condition;
  and
- its result can be scored deterministically.

Counts alone cannot make a group useful.

## 8. Labels and location roles

### 8.1 Required roles

Continue the V0.3 roles:

- `OBSERVATION`
- `HARNESS`
- `WITNESS`
- `VULNERABLE_IMPLEMENTATION`
- `CONTRIBUTING_IMPLEMENTATION`
- `FIX_SITE_ONLY`

The primary task target is normally `VULNERABLE_IMPLEMENTATION`.

### 8.2 Label construction

The construction pass must:

- inspect authoritative evidence without Trace candidate output;
- identify the vulnerable revision;
- distinguish every touched file from accepted vulnerability locations;
- record alternative accepted targets where genuinely equivalent;
- identify observations, harnesses, witnesses, and fix-only sites;
- construct matched safe controls;
- identify natural hard negatives;
- record evidence strength;
- record uncertainty; and
- seal the proposed label set.

### 8.3 Controlled review

A separate pass must verify:

- repository and revision identity;
- security evidence;
- primary location role;
- target path, symbol, and region where available;
- vulnerable/fixed semantics;
- safe controls;
- hard-negative paths;
- finding-input leakage;
- matching rule;
- correction history; and
- inclusion in primary metrics.

The review pass must not inspect Trace ranking output.

### 8.4 Label states

- `ACCEPTED`
- `ACCEPTED_WITH_MULTIPLE_TARGETS`
- `AMBIGUOUS_EXCLUDED_FROM_PRIMARY_METRICS`
- `LABEL_EVIDENCE_INSUFFICIENT`
- `RIGHTS_REJECTED`
- `PROVENANCE_REJECTED`
- `INDEPENDENCE_REJECTED`
- `RETIRED_AFTER_CORRECTION`

Rejected and ambiguous cases remain visible in the intake report so the corpus
does not hide selection losses.

## 9. Hard negatives and safe controls

### 9.1 Natural hard-negative families

The pilot should seek:

- test or reproduction harness versus production implementation;
- observed crash frame versus upstream vulnerable source;
- sanitizer or validation sink versus unsafe producer;
- fix-only file versus vulnerable implementation;
- adjacent safe function with similar names or tokens;
- stale path or symbol from another revision;
- generated, vendored, example, fixture, or documentation lookalike;
- dependency-name match without affected implementation;
- same CWE vocabulary in unrelated code; and
- multiple plausible locations with only one evidence-supported role.

### 9.2 Safe controls

Required control classes include:

- fixed revision paired with a vulnerable revision;
- already-safe implementation;
- unrelated code with similar vocabulary;
- malformed finding;
- unsupported finding or repository content;
- insufficient-evidence finding;
- absent or stale reported path;
- safe harness or test reference;
- provenance failure;
- repository mutation; and
- optional reproduction witness mismatch.

### 9.3 Balance and reporting

The intake must not silently discard safe cases merely because they lower
headline performance.

Reports must show:

- positive group count;
- safe-control count;
- hard-negative count;
- ambiguous and rejected count;
- vulnerable/fixed pair count;
- control distribution by repository;
- false-positive counts; and
- strata with no negative controls.

## 10. Finding-input contract

### 10.1 Natural input

The primary runner input should represent the information reasonably available
to a user who already has a finding, advisory, crash report, or alert.

It must not include:

- fixing diff;
- accepted target labels;
- reviewer notes;
- hidden ground truth;
- post-fix-only symbols;
- evaluator-only taxonomy;
- result-derived hints; or
- a path inserted solely to make the ranker succeed.

### 10.2 Cue-availability metadata

Record whether the finding includes:

- exact vulnerable path;
- non-vulnerable observation or harness path;
- exact target symbol;
- non-target symbol;
- source region;
- stack or witness location;
- CWE;
- repository/version information;
- vulnerability description; and
- reproduction evidence.

### 10.3 Diagnostic cue ablations

After the primary unchanged baseline is sealed, development-only transformed
variants may remove:

- reported path;
- symbol;
- region;
- CWE;
- high-information identifiers; or
- observation text.

Ablations are diagnostic and must not count as independent natural groups.
They must never cross into qualification or inflate readiness counts.

## 11. Repository-disjoint splits

### 11.1 Split units

Split at repository-lineage level, not group level.

Treat these as one lineage where applicable:

- forks;
- mirrors;
- release branches;
- vendored copies;
- common benchmark templates;
- transformed variants;
- repositories sharing the relevant vulnerable component; and
- repeated disclosures of the same defect.

### 11.2 Pilot partitions

- `construction`
- `development`
- `qualification`
- `frozen_holdback`

The pilot may also record `future_training_candidate`, but that label means
only that later review is possible.

Partition assignment must occur after rights and lineage precheck and before
reserved label or result exposure.

### 11.3 Qualification minimum

A qualification run may occur only if:

- more than one independent repository is present in qualification;
- development and qualification contain relevant positive and safe controls;
- no repository lineage crosses partitions;
- qualification labels remain evaluator-only;
- the unchanged baseline and thresholds are sealed;
- the run budget is fixed; and
- the corpus report states that the small pilot cannot support a broad product
  performance claim.

If those conditions cannot be met, qualification must remain unrun.

### 11.4 Protected holdback

V0.3.1 does not open the protected holdback.

Any repository reserved as a future holdback must remain:

- `FROZEN_UNOPENED`;
- absent from development tooling;
- unavailable to the runner outside an authorised frozen workflow;
- excluded from training eligibility; and
- represented publicly only by non-sensitive counts and identities.

## 12. Baseline execution

### 12.1 Pre-run seal

Before execution, seal:

- runtime artifact and hash;
- evaluator artifact and hash;
- environment lock;
- registry;
- repository rights records;
- split manifest;
- label-package identity;
- metric specification;
- runner configuration;
- top-k cutoffs;
- resource limits;
- output root; and
- development run budget.

### 12.2 Runner blindness

The runner may receive only:

- governed finding;
- immutable repository snapshot;
- top-k and resource configuration; and
- optional separately authorised reproduction plan.

It must not receive labels, fixing diffs, safe/vulnerable state, hard-negative
paths, reviewer notes, or reserved partition metadata beyond what execution
policy requires.

### 12.3 Raw sealing and scoring

For every run:

1. verify all input identities;
2. execute in a disposable workspace;
3. retain failures as case results;
4. seal raw outputs;
5. make labels available only to the scoring process;
6. calculate locked metrics;
7. verify the package;
8. replay the required sample; and
9. retain resource observations separately from canonical identities where
   required.

## 13. Metrics

### 13.1 Primary safety metrics

- false-vulnerability rate on safe controls;
- false `SUPPORTED` count and rate;
- wrong-location-role top-one count and rate;
- hard-negative outrank count and rate;
- unsafe non-abstention count and rate;
- unsupported-input acceptance count and rate; and
- false `CONFIRMED` reproduction count and rate where applicable.

### 13.2 Retrieval metrics

- target indexability;
- file Recall@1, @5, @10, and @20;
- symbol Recall@1, @5, @10, and @20;
- region Recall@1, @5, @10, and @20;
- role-correct Recall@1, @5, @10, and @20;
- mean reciprocal rank;
- first accepted rank;
- maximum-depth target coverage;
- no-relevant-candidate rate; and
- accepted target versus hard-negative score margin.

### 13.3 Disposition and abstention

- vulnerable/safe confusion matrix;
- precision and recall;
- safe-control specificity;
- abstention coverage;
- selective accuracy;
- insufficient-evidence agreement;
- supported-evidence completeness; and
- disposition agreement by repository.

### 13.4 Aggregation

Report:

- group micro;
- repository macro;
- repository-family macro;
- vulnerable versus safe;
- language;
- CWE;
- repository-size band;
- location role;
- hard-negative family;
- label evidence source;
- full-cue versus cue-limited findings;
- vulnerable/fixed pair; and
- natural versus transformed diagnostic origin.

Repository macro, false-positive controls, and role-correct top-one performance
are primary decision views.

### 13.5 Resource metrics

- repository acquisition bytes;
- snapshot and index time;
- scoring time;
- wall-clock and CPU time;
- peak resident memory;
- workspace size;
- retained evidence size;
- file, token, symbol, and candidate counts;
- cache state;
- timeout and resource-limit failures; and
- optional Docker setup and execution cost.

No GPU metric is required.

## 14. Threshold and decision policy

### 14.1 Development threshold decision

Development evidence may support:

- approval of provisional pilot thresholds;
- rejection of the current deterministic ranker;
- a request for more groups;
- a deterministic remediation proposal; or
- a later learned-reranker proposal.

Thresholds must be recorded before qualification.

### 14.2 Fixed integrity floors

- zero protected-holdback exposure;
- zero unauthorised repository acquisition;
- zero cross-split lineage overlap;
- zero label leakage into runner inputs;
- zero retained-evidence verification failure;
- zero malformed-input false `SUPPORTED` result;
- zero negative-control false `CONFIRMED` reproduction;
- exact same-host identity agreement for identity-bearing outputs; and
- zero third-party source content in public evidence.

### 14.3 Performance interpretation

The pilot is too small for a broad market or product claim.

It may establish:

- whether natural corpus construction is feasible;
- whether deterministic target coverage is adequate;
- whether reported-location overtrust is systematic;
- whether false positives are controllable;
- whether location-role labels are operational;
- which strata fail; and
- whether scaling or a model experiment is justified.

It may not establish general vulnerability-discovery capability.

## 15. Deterministic remediation boundary

If the unchanged baseline fails, diagnose before changing behavior.

### 15.1 Indexability failure

Consider:

- language support;
- encoding;
- generated or vendored exclusion policy;
- symbol extraction;
- size and token budgets;
- archive and repository materialisation; and
- target representation.

### 15.2 Ranking failure

Consider:

- observation versus implementation semantics;
- test/harness path treatment;
- path and symbol overtrust;
- score-reason balance;
- safe-control features;
- location-role awareness; and
- evidence-pair relationships.

### 15.3 Disposition failure

Consider:

- insufficient-evidence policy;
- fixed-revision recognition;
- safe-control representation;
- unsupported input handling;
- score calibration limitations; and
- whether deterministic rules can express the required distinction.

### 15.4 Change control

Any remediation must:

- use development data only;
- receive a new algorithm and runtime identity;
- preserve the unchanged baseline;
- rerun deterministic and leakage tests;
- be compared on the same development package;
- lock before qualification; and
- avoid task-specific rules that encode individual labels.

## 16. Model-decision boundary

V0.3.1 must not train or acquire a model.

A later learned-reranker brief becomes justified only if natural evidence shows:

- acceptable target indexability;
- the accepted target is usually present within the deterministic candidate
  set;
- ordering, role selection, safe discrimination, or abstention remains the
  principal error;
- labels and controls are sufficiently reliable;
- a learned component has a narrower role than the whole Trace pipeline; and
- deterministic remediation alone is unlikely to meet the approved threshold.

The first proposed model role should be one of:

- candidate reranker;
- vulnerable/safe discriminator;
- location-role classifier;
- evidence-pair scorer; or
- safe-abstention classifier.

The proposal must retain deterministic provenance, policy, execution, sealing,
and decision authority.

## 17. Trace IR boundary

V0.3.1 preserves:

`IR_FEASIBILITY_SUPPORTED`

It does not:

- add episodes;
- acquire public telemetry;
- change IR ranking;
- add integrations;
- run a live collector;
- add response actions;
- train an event model; or
- make an attack-detection claim.

Trace IR artifacts must remain separate from the Trace Code natural corpus.

## 20. Work packages

### WP0 - Authority and storage preflight

- verify V0.3 evidence;
- confirm clean F:/G: roots;
- create or verify governed intake, repository, manifest, and run stores;
- confirm no C: build or corpus path;
- record acquisition authority and exclusions;
- preserve Trace IR and holdback states; and
- seal the preflight.

**Exit:** The programme can acquire only approved public repositories into
governed private storage.

### WP1 - Intake register and rights precheck

- identify candidate projects;
- record upstream, revision, licence, security evidence, language, CWE, and
  lineage;
- reject unclear rights or evidence;
- approve a bounded acquisition list; and
- record rejected candidates and reasons.

**Exit:** Every planned fetch has an `ACQUISITION_APPROVED` record.

### WP2 - Inert acquisition and repository admission

- acquire exact revisions without executing repository behavior;
- hash transport artifacts;
- materialise safe snapshots;
- verify licence evidence;
- record tree identities;
- identify vulnerable and fixed revisions;
- audit size, paths, links, and special files; and
- admit or reject each repository.

**Exit:** The governed store contains reproducible admitted revisions or a
precise acquisition stop record.

### WP3 - Group and label construction

- construct governed findings;
- identify accepted targets and location roles;
- create vulnerable/fixed and safe-control pairs;
- identify natural hard negatives;
- record ambiguity and evidence strength;
- seal proposed labels; and
- retain construction receipts.

**Exit:** Proposed groups are independent of Trace ranking output.

### WP4 - Controlled review and corpus sufficiency

- verify rights, repository identity, labels, roles, safe controls, and
  negatives;
- resolve disagreements append-only;
- reject insufficient groups;
- calculate admitted and rejected distributions;
- assess the 50-100 group and 8-12 repository targets; and
- issue the pilot sufficiency decision.

**Exit:** The corpus is sufficient for a development baseline or closes
`DATA_GATES_PENDING`.

### WP5 - Repository-disjoint split seal

- audit forks, mirrors, vendored overlap, shared histories, and duplicate
  vulnerabilities;
- assign construction, development, qualification, and unopened holdback at
  lineage level;
- verify positive and safe-control coverage;
- seal labels and splits; and
- block cross-partition access.

**Exit:** Development may run without exposing qualification labels or the
holdback.

### WP6 - Unchanged natural development baseline

- verify the exact V0.1 wheel;
- execute the development partition;
- retain failures;
- seal raw outputs;
- score locked metrics;
- replay the required sample;
- perform cue-availability analysis;
- classify failures; and
- issue the natural development report.

**Exit:** The first natural baseline is immutable and interpretable.

### WP7 - Threshold and remediation decision

- assess integrity floors;
- assess safety, retrieval, role, disposition, and resource metrics;
- approve or decline provisional thresholds;
- choose retain, deterministic remediation, more data, or learned-reranker
  proposal;
- avoid qualification evidence; and
- seal the decision.

**Exit:** The qualification preconditions are explicit.

### WP8 - Qualification

If authorised by WP7:

- verify the sealed condition;
- execute the repository-disjoint qualification partition once under the
  approved budget;
- seal raw outputs before scoring;
- score with evaluator-only labels;
- verify and replay;
- expose qualification material only as prescribed; and
- issue the decision without retuning.

Resource controls should include:

## 24. Closure states

V0.3.1 must close in exactly one state:

### `NATURAL_PILOT_QUALIFIED / SCALE_CORPUS`

The intake, labels, controls, splits, and natural baseline are trustworthy.
The deterministic result does not require immediate remediation or model work,
and the programme should scale toward the full readiness corpus.

### `NATURAL_BASELINE_ESTABLISHED / TRACE_001_DESIGN_REQUIRED`

Natural indexability and candidate coverage are adequate, but role selection,
candidate ordering, safe discrimination, or abstention provides a clear,
bounded justification for a learned-model proposal. Training remains
unauthorised.

### `DETERMINISTIC_REMEDIATION_REQUIRED`

Snapshot, index, finding semantics, ranking, or deterministic disposition
failures must be corrected before a model experiment is meaningful.

### `MORE_NATURAL_DATA_REQUIRED`

The admitted pilot is valid but too small, unbalanced, or uncertain to support
a threshold or architecture decision.

### `DATA_GATES_PENDING`

Rights-cleared repositories or controlled-reviewed groups remain below the
minimum required to run a trustworthy baseline.

### `NOT_QUALIFIED / REMEDIATION_REQUIRED`

Acquisition, rights, leakage, split, label, evaluator, metric, replay, or
public-boundary defects invalidate the decision evidence.

No state authorises training or holdback opening.

## 25. Acceptance criteria

V0.3.1 is complete when:

1. V0.3 evidence and the exact V0.1 runtime verify.
2. Acquisition and corpus work remain on governed F:/G: roots, outside C:.
3. Every fetched repository has prior approval, immutable identity, licence
   evidence, and an acquisition receipt.
4. Repository-controlled behavior is never executed during intake.
5. The admitted pilot contains 8 to 12 unrelated repositories and 50 to 100
   accepted groups, or records a valid data insufficiency closure.
6. Evaluation, retention, reporting, redistribution, and future-training rights
   are distinct.
7. Vulnerable/fixed pairs, safe controls, hard negatives, rejected cases, and
   ambiguity are reported.
8. Labels distinguish every V0.3 location role and remain independent of Trace
   output.
9. Repository lineages do not cross partitions.
10. The protected holdback remains unopened.
11. The unchanged V0.1 natural development baseline is sealed before
    remediation.
12. False positives, wrong-role top-one results, hard-negative outrank, and
    unsafe non-abstention are primary metrics.
13. Cue-ablation diagnostics do not inflate natural group counts.
14. Thresholds are approved or declined before qualification.
15. Qualification, if run, is repository-disjoint, label-blind, single-budget,
    sealed, verified, and not used for retuning.
16. No model is acquired or trained.
17. Trace IR remains unchanged and separate.
18. Public evidence contains no third-party source or case-level protected
    substance.
19. Training readiness is evaluated gate by gate.
20. The closure uses one state from section 24.

## 26. Direction after V0.3.1

The result determines the next build:

- Strong natural deterministic performance:
  scale corpus construction toward 500 groups and 25 repositories before
  adding a model.
- Strong indexability but weak ordering or role selection:
  prepare a separate, narrowly scoped micro-reranker build brief.
- Weak indexability:
  improve deterministic repository and language representation.
- Unsafe false-positive or non-abstention profile:
  expand safe controls and redesign discrimination before training.
- Insufficient rights-cleared data:
  continue governed acquisition without weakening admission standards.
- Valid Code baseline and continuing market need for attack evidence:
  prepare a separate Trace IR natural-telemetry feasibility brief.

The immediate objective is not to prove that a micro model is needed. It is to
produce the first natural evidence capable of making that decision.
